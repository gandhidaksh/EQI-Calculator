How the score is computed

Each raw concentration reading is placed in its quality band and interpolated in its corresponding score range to obtain the SI score in the defined index. The formula used depends on the parameter:

Eq 1 SI=L+ (U−L)×(C−CL) CU−CL (1)
Eq 2 SI=L+ (U−L)×(CU−C) CU−CL (2)
Eq 3 SI=L+ (U−L)×(logC−logCL) logCU−logCL (3)
Where: SI: sub-index score of the parameter C: measured concentration  |  CL, CU: lower and upper bounds of its band L, U: lower and upper score bounds of that band
  • Eq 1: used for BOD, COD, TSS, TN, TP, the alkaline side of pH, and FC within the Good band (1–100 MPN/100 mL).
  • Eq 2: the acidic side of pH. pH is the only parameter that can be bad in either direction; below 7.0 the interpolation runs in reverse, since a lower pH is worse. Its change is reported as a shift toward neutral, not a removal %.
  • Eq 3: FC above the Good band. Faecal coliform spans several orders of magnitude, so each concentration term is log-transformed before interpolating. For the same reason FC reduction is reported as log removal, log10(inlet ÷ outlet), rather than a percentage.

Combining the seven sub-indices:

  • Bands and limits derive from the Environment (Protection) Rules, 1986: Schedule VI (MoEFCC, general standards for discharge of effluents; if the CPCB link is slow to load, search "Environment Protection Rules 1986 Schedule VI") and the NGT order in Nitin Shankar Deshpande v. Union of India (2018).
  • A parameter complies when its sub-index is ≤ 100, i.e. it is within the CPCB limit. A sub-index of ≤ 50 means it also meets the stricter NGT limit. Anything above 100 (Moderate or worse) is non-compliant.
  • If every parameter complies, the overall EQI is the maximum of the seven sub-indices (the plant is graded on its worst-performing parameter).
  • If any parameter fails, the overall EQI is the weighted mean of the non-compliant parameters only (so a serious breach cannot be diluted by good numbers elsewhere).
