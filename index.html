How the score is computed

Most raw readings are placed in their quality band and linearly interpolated into a 0–500 sub-index:
index = index_low + (reading − limit_low) / (limit_high − limit_low) × (index_high − index_low)

Two parameters are handled differently:

  • FC — logarithmic. Faecal coliform in sewage and effluent varies across several orders of magnitude (the scale runs 1 to 100,000 MPN/100 mL), so a linear fit inside a band would distort the score. FC is interpolated in log space instead:
    index = index_low + (index_high − index_low) × (log C − log C_low) / (log C_high − log C_low)
  • pH — two-sided. pH is the only parameter where a reading can be bad in either direction. Readings at or above 7.0 are graded on the alkaline bands; readings below 7.0 are graded on the acidic bands, where the interpolation runs in reverse because a lower pH is worse. Since pH cannot be "removed", its change is reported as a shift toward or away from neutral rather than a removal %.

Combining the seven sub-indices:

  • Bands and limits are taken directly from the NGT / CPCB reference table.
  • A parameter complies when its sub-index is ≤ 100 (Good or Satisfactory).
  • If every parameter complies, the overall EQI is the equal-weighted mean of all seven. If any parameter fails, the EQI is the mean of only the failing parameters — so a single serious breach cannot be hidden by good numbers elsewhere.
